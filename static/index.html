About ZONAR

Understanding the project — explained simply

💡

What is ZONAR?

Imagine you're trying to talk to your friend in a noisy cafeteria. The louder the noise, the harder it is to hear each other. Now imagine that's happening underwater — but instead of you and your friend, it's a mother whale trying to find her baby.

Every day, 60,000+ cargo ships cross the world's oceans. Their engines produce a deep rumbling noise that travels hundreds of kilometers underwater. This noise sits in the exact same frequency range that whales use to communicate (20–200 Hz). It's like someone blasting a loudspeaker right next to your ear — 24/7.

ZONAR is like a speed camera for the ocean — but instead of catching cars going too fast, it catches ships being too loud, and tells them to quiet down using international law and AI.

🔊

What is Acoustic Masking?

Ship engine noise (20-200Hz) directly overlaps with whale communication bands. This is called acoustic masking — the ship noise literally drowns out the voices of marine animals. Whales can't find their calves. Sea turtles lose their navigation sense. Entire ecosystems collapse.

👻

What are Ghost Vessels?

Up to 30% of ships intentionally turn off their AIS tracking transponders so nobody knows they're there. These are called Ghost Vessels — they cause all the acoustic damage with zero accountability. ZONAR detects them through their sound alone.
